Sources, leads, conflicts

Research and records

Confirmed means a record states it. Probable means the records point to it and a certificate would make it firmer. Family tradition is labeled as such. A parent a record names is on the tree with a dashed line. A name that appears only in a compiled book, or a place that is only a lead, stays on this page and is not drawn.

Open leads

  • George Magwire and Mary Prentiss are on the tree with a dashed line. Albert's 1887 marriage card and 1929 death card name them as his parents, and nothing earlier has been found. His 1887 marriage was his second, and his first wife is unknown. A Brooklyn birth also points away from the Brandon, Vermont, Magwire family (John Magwire and Francis G. Magwire), which is not on the tree.
  • Nathaniel Spalding, named in the Spalding Memorial (1872) as the father of Charles Elliot Spaulding, and the longer pedigree in that book back toward Edward Spalding. Compiled genealogy only.
  • Timothy Putnam of Charlestown, New Hampshire, and Betsey (Hall) Putnam, named in the Dickey Genealogy (1898) as the parents of Amantha Putnam. The book gives Betsey a birth on May 26, 1784. Compiled genealogy only.
  • Whether Charles Pierce, named as Eva's father in 1906, is the Charles Pierce who married Alice Whitehouse at Hebron, Yarmouth County, in 1865. Eva's mother Alice may have had the maiden name Whitehouse. Both are unproved. The 1865 register.
  • When and where Harry James Farr and Laura Mary (Seward) Farr were divorced. His 1926 marriage card says second marriage, divorced, so the marriage ended between 1912 and 1926. Divorce papers are the next record to look for.
  • Whether the Harry James Farr who married in Springfield in 1912 is the Harry J. Farr born in Denver in 1888. Same name, same town, and the age fits. Still probable until the 1912 card is read.
  • George Oscar Farr, born in Westfield, Massachusetts, and Alice Beard, born in Andover, Vermont, named as Harry James Farr's parents. William Wallace West and Alice Dina Waite, born in Dorset, Vermont, named as Mary Abbie West's parents. One or two records each. No more is known.
  • Harold Charles Magwire has a Social Security claims index entry and a death index entry on Ancestry that have not been opened. His death in 2002 rests on a Find a Grave memorial for now.
  • The 1882 marriage index reads James H. Crosby. Ernest's 1906 marriage says James W. The 1937 death registration says James William. One man is the best reading. A census of James, Asenath and Ernest in one household would close it.
  • Family tradition: summer visits to a West farm in Wells, Maine. The place table marks it possible, so it is not on the map.

Conflicts

The husband's name

The tree follows the research files and calls Tracy's husband Richard "Rick" R. Crosby. Roy E. Crosby's 2014 obituary and Dorothy's 2022 obituary list their children as Robert, Debra, Rick and Lynn. Howard Magwire's memorial says "Tracy Crosby (Richard)". Gwendolyn (Farr) Magwire's 2009 obituary names her son-in-law as Richard Crosby. A family account has also used the name Robert Richardson Crosby for Tracy's husband. The records point to Richard, called Rick, and to Robert as his brother. The middle initial R. may stand for Richardson, his mother's maiden name, which is not proved. The question stays open until Sebastian or the family says, or until a marriage record is read. No addresses or other personal details are published.

Gwendolyn's mother

Closed. Gwendolyn's Vermont birth card, July 1, 1927, names her parents as Harry J. Farr and M. Abbie West, born at Peru, Vermont. That confirms what her brother Richard's obituary said. Laura Mary Seward, married in 1912, was Harry's first wife, and the 1926 marriage card shows that marriage ended in divorce. When it ended is the open point above.

Where Albert and Alice Jenny married

Resolved toward the record. A Find a Grave note says Cavendish. The Vermont marriage card for November 8, 1887 was recorded at Springfield, so the map and the tree now put the marriage at Springfield. The note and the card disagree, and the card is the record.

Raymond's burial place

Resolved toward the physical place. Find a Grave and the older note say Hillside Cemetery, Castleton. The Veterans gravesite record says Hillside Cemetery, Fair Haven. The cemetery name agrees. The only Hillside Cemetery in Rutland County in the USGS place-name file is in Castleton, about four miles east of Fair Haven, where Raymond lived in 1940. The map therefore pins Castleton. Probable, until the cemetery's own burial register is seen.

Ernest's birth date

Minor. His 1918 draft card gives June 1, 1884. His 1942 draft card writes 1/6/1884 in a month, day, year box, which would be January 6. The older card spells the month out, so June 1 is probable.

Albert's age

Minor. The 1887 marriage card says 40, which fits a birth in October 1845 only roughly (he was 41). His 1929 death card gives October 10, 1845 and 83 years, 4 months and 11 days, which agrees. The birthplace is N.Y. City on the marriage card and Brooklyn on the death card, which fits.

Spellings

Magwire and Maguire, and Spalding and Spaulding, are spellings, not two families. Ethel is memorialized as Maguire. Edna's death index says Albert Maguire. The 1872 book spells Spalding. The family used Spaulding. Search both.

Barbara's birth day

Closed. A computer reading of the 1921 Wayland register said October 20. The printed page, and her obituary, say October 26, 1921. The page image is on the Richardson page.

Not yet on the map

The map is driven only by the research place table. A place known only roughly (a Nova Scotia village, a cemetery plot we have not measured, a place known only to its county) is a soft circle or an area, not a street pin. A fact that already names a town on the map uses that town's pin. A street address does not get a pin of its own, because the place table has no measured street point. The map's own list, "Not yet on the map," gathers dated facts that still have no place: Bernard and Beatrice's 1948 marriage, Navy and Army service with no base named, and deaths with no town named.

On November 28, 2026 the map was checked against every place the records name. Towns and villages the records give were added as soft circles, placed from OpenStreetMap: Charlestown, New Hampshire, and Montpelier for work; Westfield, Andover, Dorset, and Pleasant Lake, Lake George and South Ohio in Nova Scotia for births; Ludlow for Albert's 1904 concert; Schenectady and Hydeville for homes named in obituaries. Oakland Cemetery in Springfield and Cavendish Village Cemetery are now pins of their own. Pleasant Lake and Pleasant Valley stay two separate places.

Named in the records, and not given a pin:

  • Otis Reservoir, the summer home in Roy and Dorothy's obituaries. Family tradition.
  • Wells, Maine, the lead above.
  • Hebron, Nova Scotia, the unproved 1865 Pierce marriage, and Brandon, Vermont, the unlinked Magwire memorials.
  • Howard Magwire's stay in West Springfield by 1967. Roy and Dorothy's home there is on the map. Howard's is not a separate pin.
  • Fairview Cemetery, where Muriel (Crosby) Strauss is buried with Leonard. Her obituary does not name the town, so the cemetery is not placed.
  • William Wallace West's birthplace. His card gives Vermont, and the town is hard to read.
  • Woodstock, Vermont, where Harry Farr's 1942 draft card was registered. It is the local board's town, not his home.

Held back

  • Living people: name and relationship only. No dates, places, photographs or biographies, and no map pins. That covers Tracy, Rick, Brittany, Mitch, Travis and Alma, and it covers Nellie Magwire, Doris Ann Elliott, Rick's siblings, and anyone named only as a living cousin.
  • No contact details for anyone.
  • Full obituary texts, funeral-home photographs, and Find a Grave photographs are not in this site. The obituaries of Richard H. Farr, Gwendolyn (Farr) Magwire and Barbara R. Vogel are linked. The page images stay in the private research files.
  • Gwendolyn's guest book, which names living cousins, is not used.
  • Seller scans and other third-party pictures are not in the repository. Town registers, census pages, the two genealogies and the Nova Scotia registrations are public records and are shown with the entry marked.
